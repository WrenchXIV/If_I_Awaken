PROLOGUE

In a city of stories, no one had told this one.

In Los Angeles, things take root. People from all over the world feel this — and so they flock here. The dreamers, the wanderers, the immigrants, in search of a chance, a rebirth, a fresh start. And the city receives them.

There has never been a show that told that LA. Not the Hollywood postcard. Not the Walk of Fame. The city itself. The languages, the music, the neighborhoods, the people who actually live here.

This is the story of how that show is being built — and the chapter you're being invited into.

CHAPTER ONE · OUR HERO

A teacher in Los Angeles named Diane Luby Lane.

Diane is the founder of Get Lit — Words Ignite. For two decades she has been putting poetry in the hands of LA students. Her curriculum is now in over two hundred schools worldwide. The James Patterson Page Turner Award. The Presidential Lifetime Volunteer Service Award. LA Times, CNN, NPR.

But the curriculum was only ever a means. The real project was always the city — listening to LA tell itself, in its own voices, in its own words.

"Los Angeles has loved us. With this show, we offer that love back."

CHAPTER TWO · THE CALL TO ADVENTURE

An idea, simple enough to fit on a single page.

A tour of the real Los Angeles — not the Hollywood backdrop, but the city underneath. The audience wouldn't sit. They would walk. Through seven neighborhoods. With the actual people. With the actual music. At theatre scale.

Diane brought it to Andrew Scoville (director, Theater of the Mind; associate director, Here Lies Love; director, Money Heist: The Experience). To Derrick Hodge (two-time Grammy). To Gina Belafonte (Sankofa). To Tony-winning producers Shane Snow and Rob O'Neill.

The team said yes. Then the city had to.

CHAPTER THREE · THE FIRST TEST

August 1, 2025. The Ford Theatre.

In partnership with the LA Philharmonic. Directed by Gina. Composed by Derrick. On stage: Luis J. Rodriguez (former LA Poet Laureate), Samantha Rios (current LA Youth Poet Laureate), Dante Basco, Mendeleyev.

The line stretched around the block. The show held the curtain thirty minutes to let people in. The Ford had not seen a night like it in years.

"They didn't just speak about Los Angeles — they spoke as Los Angeles." — The Hollywood Times

CHAPTER FIVE · THE CITY ANSWERS

Then we asked one question — and the city wrote back.

"Tell us about your LA."

We had no idea what we were starting. Partnered with the Los Angeles Unified School District and Get Lit — Words Ignite, the question went into schools, classrooms, cafeterias, community centers. Judged by Aloe Blacc, José Olivarez, Olivia Gatwood, Safia Elhillo, Chen Chen, and Gina Belafonte.

By January, it had become the largest poetry competition in the country — by accident.

CHAPTER SEVEN · THE GUIDE

Every hero needs a guide. This is the part you play.

In every story, the moment the hero crosses the threshold, the world expects them to fail. They can't go alone. They need someone to hand them the sword.

The pilot is proven. The album is proven. The competition is proven. The team is proven. The only thing missing is the build.
